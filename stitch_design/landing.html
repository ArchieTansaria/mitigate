The problem

One published fix. Many customer deployments. No guarantee they all received it.

A supplier can publish a repaired component, but customer-owned contracts may still use an older implementation, have unsafe configuration, or route through a different contract than expected. A release announcement tells you a fix exists. It doesn’t tell you which deployments are repaired.

01

Version checks

Correct code can still be connected to incorrect settings.

02

One successful test

One deployment passing does not prove every customer deployment passes.

03

Manual coordination

Teams have to match advisories, addresses, owners, and repair status across separate tools.

04

PatchBond

Inspect the registered deployment, show which checks pass or fail, and keep evidence tied to the exact deployment and repair policy.

What PatchBond does

A repair workflow with evidence at each step.

01 travel_explore

Inspect the deployed path

Trace the registered application entrypoint to the dependency it actually uses.

02 difference

Explain the mismatch

Show whether the issue is an old implementation, incorrect configuration, or a failed behavior check.

03 terminal

Prepare and simulate

Build a proposed repair for an authorized owner to review. Simulate it before any real transaction.

04 verified

Verify the result

Recheck the deployment after the owner executes the repair. Report what passed, failed, or could not be established.

05 hub

Connect evidence to a campaign

A campaign can use verified evidence to decide whether a deployment meets its published conditions. Only an authorized wallet executes contract changes.
